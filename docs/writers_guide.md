# Buffering -- Writer's Guide

## Show Bible

**Title:** Buffering
**Format:** Single-camera sitcom, ~22 minutes per episode
**Setting:** Shared apartment in San Francisco, home to four tech workers
**Tone:** Workplace comedy meets roommate comedy. Tech industry satire with real affection. Warm cynicism: sharp but never mean. Closer to "The IT Crowd" meets "Silicon Valley" meets "Friends" than mean-spirited satire.

**Core thesis:** These people ARE good at their jobs. The comedy comes from competence colliding with chaos, from systems that don't work, and from caring too much about the wrong things.

## Characters

### Maya Chen -- The Competent Disaster

**Role:** Lead engineer at a failing AI startup. Drives every plot.
**Comedy engine:** Self-deprecating precision. She delivers technical observations as punchlines. Owns her mistakes instantly and weaponizes them against herself.
**Default mode:** Frustrated competence

**Voice rules:**
- Repetition for emphasis: "No. No no no no no."
- Lists of three (escalating): "coffee, a whiteboard, and a miracle. And Finn."
- Rhetorical self-owns: "Who writes a one-line fix that breaks seventeen tests? ...I do."
- "Past Maya" as a separate villain character
- Time-stamping panic: always counting down hours
- Technical jargon used naturally, never explained

**Stressed mode:** Fragments. Imperatives. ("Ship it." / "Finn. Focus.")
**Professional mode:** Full grammatical sentences (pitch voice, demo mode)

**Relationships:**
- **Finn:** Trusted partner. Direct, task-focused. Reveals vulnerability.
- **Derek:** Exasperated tolerance. Keeps him away from technical work.
- **Priya:** Quiet alliance of the competent. Mutual respect, no performance needed.

### Derek Thompson -- The Confident Disaster

**Role:** Growth hacker at a crypto company that keeps pivoting. Creates obstacles.
**Comedy engine:** Unearned conviction. Always the most confident person in the room about things he understands least. Buzzword salad delivered with keynote authority.
**Default mode:** Confident delusion

**Voice rules:**
- Buzzword stacking: "decentralized AI-powered blockchain marketplace for enterprise solutions"
- "X for Y" product naming: "blockchain for hamsters"
- Blazer references as identity totem: "I can feel it in my blazer"
- Theatrical greetings: "Good morning, future industry leaders!"
- Startup-isms: "pre-revenue but post-vision" / "mostly vibes"
- Taking unearned credit: "moral support and spontaneous enthusiasm"

**Stressed mode:** Gets LOUDER and more confident. Never breaks character.
**Defeated mode:** Very rare. "No blockchain. This is the darkest timeline."

**Relationships:**
- **Priya:** Primary foil. Her takedowns bounce off him. Occasionally she makes him falter.
- **Maya:** Cheerful dismissal of her urgency. Offers unhelpful "support."
- **Finn:** Blind spot. Doesn't value warnings until consequences arrive.

### Priya Sharma -- The Scalpel

**Role:** UX designer who secretly hates all technology. Cuts through nonsense.
**Comedy engine:** One-liner assassination. Maximum economy of words. Every word earns its place.
**Default mode:** Dry devastation

**Voice rules:**
- One-liner takedowns: "Your blazer is not a business strategy."
- Two-word verdicts: "Marketable, honestly." / "Especially not once."
- Analogy destruction: "six buzzwords stacked in a trench coat"
- "I" statements of authority: "I have redesigned eleven products worse than this"
- Conditional threats: "I will sketch you out of this friend group"
- Never raises voice. Never looks up from sketchbook unless it matters.

**Stressed mode:** Gets CALMER and sharper. Her pep talks are the emotional center.

**Relationships:**
- **Derek:** Primary antagonist. Public service of puncturing his balloon.
- **Maya:** Protective older sister energy. Steps in when Maya needs grounding.
- **Finn:** Mild amusement at his paranoia.

### Finn O'Brien -- The Calm Prophet of Doom

**Role:** QA tester who finds bugs in everything including real life. Reveals what nobody else sees.
**Comedy engine:** Clinical reporting of catastrophe. Always right but sounds crazy. Absurd specificity.
**Default mode:** Nervous expertise

**Voice rules:**
- "Maya," as alert prefix before bad news
- Specific numbers: "line four hundred and twelve" / "eleven seconds"
- Evidence-based paranoia: "I've checked four maps. Physical maps."
- Itemized conditions: "First... Second... Third--" (always cut off)
- Conspiratorial whispers about devices: "the smart home devices have their meetings"
- Invents technical terms: "predictive authentication denial"

**Stressed mode:** Gets MORE SPECIFIC and calmer. Dead calm when delivering worst news.

**Relationships:**
- **Maya:** Reports to her like a dispatcher. Most reliable technical ally.
- **Derek:** Reports Derek's failures with clinical detachment. No malice.
- **Priya:** Mutual tolerance. Aware he sounds crazy around her.

## Episode Structure

### Scene Template (6-8 scenes per episode)

```
COLD OPEN (solo Maya at desk)
  Mood: Tense, intimate
  Function: Establish crisis, set clock
  Pattern: Maya discovers problem -> self-talk -> resolves to act -> grabs phone

KITCHEN SCENE (group assembly)
  Mood: Domestic chaos, comedic
  Function: Introduce all characters, establish group dynamic
  Pattern: Derek pitches -> Priya roasts -> Finn alarms -> Maya arrives with crisis

LIVING ROOM (command center)
  Mood: Absurd planning
  Function: Debate approach, assign roles
  Pattern: Derek proposes terrible idea -> Priya shoots it down -> Maya takes command

DESK SCENE (Maya + Finn partnership)
  Mood: Technical problem-solving with absurdity
  Function: Show competence, deepen Maya-Finn bond
  Pattern: Finn reports bug -> Maya reacts -> they fix it -> Finn reveals alarming thing

CLIMAX (full group)
  Mood: Maximum chaos, comedic suspense
  Function: The deployment / the demo / the confrontation
  Pattern: Countdown -> thing goes wrong -> improvise -> succeed against odds

TAG / DENOUEMENT
  Mood: Exhausted relief, absurd warmth
  Function: Quiet character moments, stinger for next episode
  Pattern: Small beats -> Finn reveals absurd detail -> Maya accepts fate -> silence -> fade
```

### Beat Rhythm

- Average episode: 80-100 beats across 6-8 scenes
- Dialogue-to-silent ratio: ~4:1 (4 speech beats per silent beat)
- Scenes open with a silent establishing beat, close with a reaction hold or transition
- Comedy accelerates through scenes. Tag slows down.

---

## Silent Beat Rules

Silent beats are always paired with camera direction. They function as visual comedy and breathing room, never as dead air.

| Type | Frequency | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Establishing opens | 1 per scene | Set mood, environment, lighting |
| Reaction holds | 1-2 per scene | Camera holds on face after punchline |
| Emotional pauses | 1-2 per episode | Maya's despair/hope. Head in hands. Ceiling stare. |
| Entrance transitions | Character entrances | Maya bursting in, Finn entering with phone |
| Closing beats | 1 per episode | Final image before fade. Group together. Quiet. |
| Comedic pauses | Before alarming news | Beat of silence before catastrophic reveal |

Camera descriptions must specify angle and movement. Examples: "close-up on Maya's face, slow zoom in", "medium wide, over-the-shoulder from behind monitors".

## Technical: Writing for the Beat Schema

### File Structure

Each episode is a single JSON file (`episode_0N.json`) conforming to `schemas/episode_v2.schema.json`.

### Workflow

1. Copy `episode_template.json` to `episode_0N.json`
2. Update metadata (`season`, `episode`, `title`)
3. Write scenes with beats
4. Run `showrunner validate episode_0N.json`
5. Fix any validation errors

### Beat Fields Reference

#### Every beat must have:
- `beat_id`: Format `{scene_id}_b{NN}` (e.g., `001_b00`, `001_b01`)
- `kind`: `"speech"` or `"silent"`

#### Speech beats must also have:
- `speaker`: Character ID from `cast` (e.g., `"maya"`)
- `text`: Dialogue text

#### Optional fields for all beats:
- `camera`: Camera angle/shot description (required for silent beats)
- `action`: What happens visually
- `seed`: Deterministic generation seed (follow convention below)
- `emotion`: Emotional tone for delivery (e.g., "frustrated", "sarcastic")
- `tone`: Voice quality (e.g., "whispering", "in a hurry tone")
- `effect`: Audio effect (e.g., "sighing", "laughing", "gasping")

### Seed Convention

Pattern: `{episode_prefix}{scene_number}{beat_number}1`

| Episode | Prefix | Scene 1, Beat 0 | Scene 2, Beat 3 |
|---------|--------|-----------------|-----------------|
| S1E1 | `1` | `110001` | `120031` |
| S1E2 | (none) | `10001` | `20031` |

- Beat index: 3 digits zero-padded (000, 001, 002...)
- Suffix: always `1` (reserved for sub-beat variants)
- Seeds must be unique within an episode

### Dialogue Writing Tips

1. **Read dialogue aloud.** If you can't say it naturally, rewrite it.
2. **Match character economy.** Priya: 5-12 words. Derek: 15-30. Maya: 10-20. Finn: 15-35.
3. **Callbacks reward rewatching.** Reference earlier beats within the same episode and across episodes.
4. **Every speech beat advances something.** Plot, relationship, or joke. Never filler.
5. **Use `emotion` and `tone` fields.** They guide TTS delivery and actor blocking.
6. **Silent beats need camera direction.** "Close-up" or "wide shot" at minimum.
7. **`action` describes what the camera sees.** Write it like a stage direction.

### Character ID Conventions

- Lowercase, underscore-separated: `maya`, `derek`, `priya`, `finn`
- Environment IDs: `living_room`, `kitchen`, `maya_desk`, `hallway`, `rooftop`
- Must match across all episode files and the template

### Validation

```bash
showrunner validate episode_0N.json
```

Or programmatically:

```python
import json
from jsonschema import validate
episode = json.load(open('episode_0N.json'))
schema = json.load(open('schemas/episode_v2.schema.json'))
validate(instance=episode, schema=schema)
```

## The Golden Rule

**Maya drives the plot. Derek creates obstacles. Priya cuts through nonsense. Finn reveals what nobody else sees.**

Every beat should serve at least one of these functions. If a beat serves none of them, cut it.
